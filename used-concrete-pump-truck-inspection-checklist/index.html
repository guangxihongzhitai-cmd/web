What to ask · what to verify · what not to assume

Buying a used concrete pump truck from China: inspection & export checklist | Hongzhitai

A used concrete pump truck export deal usually fails for two reasons: the machine’s condition was not verified, or commercial/shipping terms were left vague. This checklist is for overseas contractors and dealers working with Guangxi Hongzhitai Import & Export Trading Co., Ltd. (Hongzhitai / HZT), a Guangxi-based exporter of used and new heavy machinery including concrete pump trucks. It is a process checklist, not a stock list and not a country-by-country import guide.

Before you inquire

Send a short brief so sales can match a real unit instead of a generic brochure. Public unit pages are on current listings — treat those as starting points, not a promise that a truck is still in the yard.

  • Boom class you need (state a range if you are flexible).
  • Brand preference if any: SANY, Zoomlion, LiuGong, or XCMG — availability depends on current units, not a standing catalog.
  • Destination country and port.
  • Timeline (when you need the machine, not a guaranteed shipping date).
  • Contact: WhatsApp +86 135 5771 6777, sales@hongzhtaichina.com, or the site contact form.

Matching a real unit

Match the quote to one machine. Do not mix photos from one truck with specs from another.

  • Ask for current photos and, if useful, video of that unit — not a gallery shot of a similar boom.
  • Confirm brand, boom, chassis, and year as offered for that unit.
  • Ask whether it is still available before you plan inspection or payment.
  • Do not rely on old screenshots or cached listing copy.
  • VIN and price are omitted on public pages — request them commercially if you need them. This page is not a price list.

Inspection checklist (used boom pump)

Inspection method is arranged case by case: yard visit, third-party inspector, or video walk-around. Say which unit you mean and which method you prefer. The lists below are what to ask and verify — they are not a claim that every item has already been done on every truck.

Identity & configuration

  • Brand and boom class on the machine match the written offer.
  • Chassis as stated for that unit — do not assume a chassis brand that is not written.
  • Year as stated; if it is not written, ask.
  • Hours: ask for this unit. Hours are not published as a public stock figure.
  • Visible layout (outriggers, hopper, remote) matches current photos of this unit.

Structure & boom

  • Current photos of boom sections, boom rest, turret, and outriggers (stowed and, if shown, deployed).
  • Ask to see any visible cracks, patches, or wet hydraulic lines in photos or on video.
  • If the agreed method allows it, a boom unfold/fold or outrigger deploy demonstration.
  • Do not assume a rebuild or “like new” boom unless it is photographed, demonstrated, or written.

Pumping unit

  • Hopper, agitator, S-valve / swing tube, delivery pipes, and water system as visible on this unit.
  • Close-ups of hydraulic hoses and joints around the pump — ask if they are not in the first photo set.
  • Whether a pumping demonstration is included in the inspection method (case by case).
  • Do not assume output, remaining wear-part life, or hours.

Truck & drivetrain

  • Engine start and idle on video or in person.
  • Drive / gearbox as demonstrated under the agreed method.
  • Current photos of tyres, lights, and cabin.
  • Do not assume destination-country roadworthiness or registration outcome.

Documents & history

Ask what exists for this unit. Do not assume a file you have not been shown.

  • Ask which documents the seller can provide for this unit, in writing.
  • Do not assume service history, licence scans, or customs-declaration copies.
  • VIN / chassis ID may be omitted publicly — request commercially if you need it.

Rule: if it is not photographed, demonstrated, or written, do not assume it.

Commercial terms

Leave terms in writing before money moves. There is no public price list on this site.

  • Identify the unit in the quotation (brand / boom / chassis / year as offered, plus any internal unit ID sales uses).
  • Inspection method and who pays for it — in writing.
  • Shipping basis (for example FOB or CIF) discussed per shipment, not as a blanket rate for every destination.
  • Payment milestones in writing.
  • Do not treat a chat screenshot as the contract.

Export paperwork

Typical export paperwork is confirmed per deal in writing. This page names only generic commercial shipping documents. It is not a destination import guide and does not publish document scans.

  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Bill of lading

Not claimed on this page:

  • Customs-declaration imagery
  • Licence scans
  • Destination-country clearances
  • Guaranteed lead times

Red flags

Walk away from a used-pump export story that depends on any of the following.

  • No current photos of the specific unit.
  • Specs that change between messages (boom, year, chassis).
  • Pressure to skip inspection.
  • Customs-declaration imagery or licence scans used as marketing.
  • “#1” / always-in-stock claims in place of a named unit.
